Before anything

Mac or Windows?

Pick the computer you're putting the AI on. Every step, every click and every prompt below changes to match it. Switch any time, here or up top.

You're reading the Mac version of this guide.

Read this first. Seriously.

You're about to let an AI run commands on your computer, then install a model with the safety filter ripped out. My prompts cut the risk way down. They don't make it zero, and nobody's supervising you but you.

  • Adults only, your own computer only. Not a work laptop, not a machine with stuff you can't afford to lose. Back up first.
  • An uncensored model is not a smart one. It has no safety filter and it's confidently wrong a lot. Don't lean on it for medical, legal, drug, chemical or crisis stuff. What you do with its answers is on you, and the law still applies.
  • The model file comes from a community publisher. The checks in this guide make a bad file much less likely. They can't prove a file is safe.
  • Always pick "allow once". Never "always allow", never auto or full-access mode. Being asked every time is the seatbelt. (In Claude, "Allow once" hides under the small arrow next to the big Always allow button. Step 1 shows you.)
  • You never type commands. The AI runs them, you click "allow once". If you notice yourself typing one into the chat or a terminal, stop.

What you'll need

  • Time: about 1 to 2 hours for steps 1 to 8, mostly waiting on downloads. The optional app in step 9 is another hour or two.
  • Downloads: Ollama is about 220 MB. Your model is 4 to 20 GB. Step 9 adds Node.js.
  • Disk space: 20 to 35 GB free, depending on your computer. Step 2 checks it for you.
  • Memory: 8 GB works for small models. 16 GB or more is where it gets good. A graphics card with 8 GB or more of its own memory helps a lot.
  • Money: one month of Claude or ChatGPT, about $20. After that, $0.

Stop signs

Say no and close the app if:

  • The AI asks for your computer password in the chat.
  • A password box pops up out of nowhere. (A couple are normal, listed below.)
  • Windows asks for your password or PIN out of nowhere.
  • A command says sudo or "admin". (The only exception: uninstalling in step 10. The AI warns you first.)
  • A box asks "Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your device?" when the AI didn't say one was coming. (Expected: the Ollama installer and the Node.js installer in step 9, and only if it shows a verified publisher.)
  • It wants to touch stuff outside your local-ai folder that the step didn't mention.
  • It downloads from anywhere but ollama.com, github.com or huggingface.co (plus nodejs.org and npmjs.com in step 9). hf.co is fine, that's just Hugging Face's short address.
  • Your Mac says an app "can't be verified" or comes from an "unknown developer".
  • A blue "Windows protected your PC" screen, or a box that says "Unknown publisher".
  • Your Mac offers to install "command line developer tools" or anything else you didn't ask for. Hit cancel.
  • Windows opens the Microsoft Store or offers to install Python, Git or anything else you didn't ask for. Close it.

Pop-ups that are fine

Not every box is a stop sign. These are normal:

  • Your Mac asking "downloaded from the Internet, are you sure?" It says that about every app.
  • A password box the first time Ollama opens, or from the Node.js installer in step 9.
  • The "Do you want to allow this app to make changes" box from the Ollama installer or the Node.js installer (step 9), as long as it shows a verified publisher with the company name the AI told you to expect.
  • Windows Firewall asking if Ollama (or Node.js in step 9) can use your networks. Click Cancel. Your AI only talks to your own computer, so it doesn't need that.
  • Ollama's "Create an account" box. Click No thanks, I'll use Ollama locally. Never Sign up.
  • Claude or ChatGPT asking to use your Desktop folder in step 8.
  • A small Terminal window when you double-click Start My AI or Stop My AI. It shows the update check, then you can close it.
  • A small black window when you double-click Start My AI or Stop My AI. It shows the update check, then you can close it.
  • Your Mac asking if one app can "control" Ollama or Finder in step 8 or 10, but only if the AI told you it was coming.

The one to stop at: "can't be verified" or "unknown developer".

The ones to stop at: "Windows protected your PC" or "Unknown publisher".

What's under the hood?

Before we download anything, we need to know what your computer can handle without crying. This step is look, don't touch. The AI only reads info about your machine. It doesn't install or change a thing.

The one rule

The model plus its memory of your chat has to fit in 80% of your RAM. The chat memory (called "context") gets 50% of the model's size. Leaves breathing room so your computer doesn't turn into a space heater. Got a separate graphics card? Then it's 80% of the card's own memory (VRAM) instead, because that's where the model runs fast.

The AI talks you through all of this too. Here's the cheat sheet, so nothing surprises you.

  1. Download it, don't open it yet

    When the AI tells you to, go to ollama.com/download in your normal browser. Make sure macOS is picked, then click Download manually under the box.

  2. Drag Ollama into Applications, then STOP

  3. Close the welcome window

    When Ollama opens, its window says to type ollama in a terminal. You don't need to. Just close that window. Say yes when it offers its command-line tool (that's the expected password box).

  4. Say no to the account

    A "Create an account" box may pop up, offering "faster, larger open models". Those run on Ollama's computers, not yours. Click No thanks, I'll use Ollama locally. Never Sign up.

  5. Leave ChatGPT off

    Click the llama icon in the menu bar. If the menu shows a "ChatGPT / Not installed" switch, leave it off.

  6. Send screenshots, don't describe

    When the AI asks about Ollama's menu or Settings, attach a screenshot instead of typing out what you see. For Settings, send two: the top, and scrolled down. It reads them and tells you which switches to change.

  7. Flip the right switches in Settings

    Click the llama icon in the menu bar again, then click Settings (the second line in the menu in the screenshot above).

    A switch is ON when its white circle is on the right. In dark mode on and off are the same grey, so go by the circle.

    • Sign In: don't click. You don't need an account.
    • Cloud: it's ON by default. Turn it OFF. Just the switch, no file editing.
    • Expose Ollama to the network: must be OFF. It's off by default.
    • Auto-download updates: leave it ON. Updates bring security fixes.
    • Show apps in menu: your call. Turning it off hides that confusing ChatGPT item.
    • Model location: leave it as it is. Don't click Browse.
    • Context length: leave it for now. Step 5 sets it.
    • "Start ChatGPT" and "Reset to defaults": don't click. Reset turns Cloud back on.

They never install anything by themselves, and there's no AI inside them. Just small, boring, readable scripts. Boring is good here.

  • Your model's file changed online: the copy on your computer isn't affected, so you don't have to do anything. Want the new one anyway? Don't just re-download it. Paste the Update Me prompt below into your setup AI, so the new file gets the same checks as the original.
  • Your model's page was removed: that can mean someone found a problem with it. Stop using it and run the Update Me prompt.
  • Cancelled your Claude or ChatGPT subscription? No stress. Your current model keeps working fine. Ollama updates don't need a setup AI. You only need one again if you want a newer model, or your model's page gets removed.

Now go nuts

Grow your own skills

Seven skills is a starter pack, not a ceiling. Want one for Rust, SQL, writing cover letters, or roasting your own code? Get it. Best way first:

  • Still got Claude or ChatGPT? Use that. It's way smarter than anything running on your laptop. Open a new chat in your local-ai folder and ask it to write a skill for whatever you want and save it in app/skills, following the README in there.
  • No subscription? Google it. Search something like "Rust coding skill markdown". Tons of people share theirs, mostly on GitHub. Copy the text, then paste it into Settings → Skills → add a skill.
  • Asking your own AI to write one? I wouldn't. On most laptops it's a pretty small model, so it'll write you a pretty dumb skill. Your AI is fine at using a good skill. Let a bigger brain write it.
  • Read it before you switch it on. A skill is instructions your AI follows, and stuff from the internet can hide sneaky ones. Stick to well-known repos with lots of stars, and if a skill tells it to run commands, go online or ignore your settings, bin it.

In case you're wondering

Where's voice typing?

I left it out on purpose. Voice needs a speech engine plus a Whisper model, and that's the part most likely to break the rules everything else here follows. Want it anyway? Ask your setup AI to add voice to your app in a new chat. Paste the SAFETY RULES from Prompt 09b first, and keep these in mind:

  • New code and a new model file. Both get the same checks as your main model: a known publisher, safe file types, and a SHA-256 written into MODEL-PLAN.md.
  • Don't loosen the locks to make it work. Lots of speech libraries grab extra files from the internet while they run, or need install scripts. If it only works with the Content-Security-Policy loosened or --ignore-scripts turned off, that's a no.
  • No built-in browser speech. Some browsers send your voice to their own servers to turn it into text. It has to run on your computer.
  • Mic access only for your app. Allow the microphone for 127.0.0.1 only, and delete each recording once it's text.
