Read this first. Seriously.

You're about to let an AI run commands on your computer, then install a model with the safety filter ripped out. My prompts cut the risk way down. They don't make it zero, and nobody's supervising you but you.

  • Adults only, your own computer only. Not a work laptop, not a machine with stuff you can't afford to lose. Back up first.
  • An uncensored model is not a smart one. It has no safety filter and it's confidently wrong a lot. Don't lean on it for medical, legal, drug, chemical or crisis stuff. What you do with its answers is on you, and the law still applies.
  • The model file comes from a community publisher. The checks in this guide make a bad file much less likely. They can't prove a file is safe.
  • Always pick "allow once". Never "always allow", never auto or full-access mode. Being asked every time is the seatbelt.

Stop signs

Say no and close the app if:

  • The AI asks for your computer password in the chat.
  • A password box pops up out of nowhere. (A couple are normal, listed below.)
  • A command says sudo or "admin". (Linux installs are the only exception, and the AI warns you first.)
  • It wants to touch stuff outside your local-ai folder that the step didn't mention.
  • It downloads from anywhere but ollama.com, github.com or huggingface.co (plus nodejs.org and npmjs.com in step 9). hf.co is fine, that's just Hugging Face's short address.
  • Your computer says an app "can't be verified".
  • Your computer offers to install "developer tools" or anything else you didn't ask for. Hit cancel.

Pop-ups that are fine

Not every box is a stop sign. These are normal:

  • Your Mac asking "downloaded from the Internet, are you sure?" It says that about every app.
  • A password box the first time Ollama opens on a Mac, or from the Node.js installer in step 9.
  • Claude or ChatGPT asking to use your Desktop folder in step 8.
  • Your browser asking for the microphone in step 9.
  • A Mac asking if one app can "control" Ollama or Finder in step 8 or 10, but only if the AI told you it was coming.

The one to stop at: "can't be verified" or "unknown developer".

Step one

Get yourself a helper

First things first. You need an AI that can actually do stuff on your computer, not just chat about it. Claude and ChatGPT both have desktop apps that can run commands, with your permission. So:

Before you pay for anything

Take ten seconds to check your computer can do this. Mac: Apple menu → About This Mac. Windows: Settings → System → About. You want 16 GB of memory (RAM) and about 30 GB of free disk space. 8 GB of RAM works, but it only fits a small, dumber model. On a Mac you also need macOS 14 (Sonoma) or newer, and really an Apple chip (M1 or later). Older Intel Macs run it, but painfully slowly. On Windows, 10 or 11 with the latest updates. This guide is written for Mac and Windows. The prompts handle Linux too, but only go there if you already know your way around it.

Set up Claude

  1. Download the Claude desktop app and sign in.
  2. Click the Code tab at the top left corner with the icon that looks like . Not the normal chat. The normal chat can't touch your computer.
  3. Make a new, empty folder called local-ai in your home folder (the one with your name on it) and pick it as the project folder. Skip the Desktop and Documents, since those often sync to iCloud or OneDrive.
  4. Under the message box, set permissions to Manual (older versions call it Ask permissions). Do not pick Auto, Accept edits or Bypass. We want it asking before it runs commands or changes anything. That's the whole point.
  5. Every time it asks, pick allow once. Never "always allow". Yes, it's a lot of clicking. That's the seatbelt.

Set up ChatGPT

  1. Download the Codex app and sign in with your ChatGPT account.
  2. Make a new, empty folder called local-ai in your home folder (the one with your name on it) and open it in Codex. Skip the Desktop and Documents, since those often sync to iCloud or OneDrive.
  3. Under the message box, set permissions to Ask for approval. Do not pick Approve for me or Full access. Those run commands without asking. We want it asking before it runs commands or changes anything. That's the whole point.
  4. Every time it asks, approve just that one command. Never "always allow" or "don't ask again". Yes, it's a lot of clicking. That's the seatbelt.
✓

Checkpoint. You've got an AI that can run commands on your computer, and it asks before it runs commands or changes anything. Onward.

Step two

What's under the hood?

Before we download anything, we need to know what your computer can handle without crying. This step is look, don't touch. The AI only reads info about your machine. It doesn't install or change a thing.

The one rule

The model plus its memory of your chat has to fit in 80% of your RAM. The chat memory (called "context") gets 50% of the model's size. Leaves breathing room so your computer doesn't turn into a space heater.

✓

Checkpoint. You've got a Hardware Report with your max model size. Copy it (the whole HARDWARE REPORT block). You'll paste it into the next step. Keep this chat open too, you're coming back to it.

Step three

Pick your fighter

Now the AI goes shopping for an abliterated model. That's a normal open model with the "sorry, I can't help with that" surgically removed. Same brain, way less hall monitor.

The catch: these are made by community publishers, not Google or OpenAI. So this prompt is extra paranoid about who made it and what's inside the files. It looks up what's new right now, checks everything is legit, and picks the best one your computer can run.

This one runs in the normal chat, not the Code tab or Codex. The AI reads a pile of web pages written by strangers here, and a web page can try to sneak instructions into an AI. In the normal chat it can't touch your computer, so a sneaky page has nothing to grab. Nothing gets downloaded until you've read the plan and said yes in the next step.

Step four

Drop in the engine

Back to the Code tab or Codex chat from step 2. Time to actually touch something. The AI double-checks the Model Plan you bring over, writes it into a file so nothing gets lost, then installs Ollama, the engine that runs your model. Before it installs anything, it proves the installer is the real deal and not some knockoff with a virus in its pocket.

You download the installer yourself, in your normal web browser, so your computer's built-in download checks stay on. The AI tells you exactly where to click, then double-checks the file before you open it. If your computer warns that the app "can't be verified", stop. No model gets downloaded yet. That's next.

  • Saves your Model Plan
  • Re-checks for new security holes
  • Verifies the installer is genuine
  • Only talks to your computer
  • Never asks for an account
✓

Checkpoint. Ollama is installed, checked and locked to your computer only, and there's a MODEL-PLAN.md in your local-ai folder. If any line in the Install Report says FAILED, stop here. Don't push through it.

From here on: new chat, every step

Start a fresh chat for each step from now on. Same app, same local-ai folder, and check the permission setting still says what you picked in step 1. Everything the AI needs is saved in your folder, so it loses nothing. A long chat slowly forgets its safety rules. A fresh one has them right at the top.

08

Step eight

Your on/off switch

A loaded model can eat a big chunk of your memory, so you want an easy way to turn it on and off. This step puts a My Local AI folder on your desktop with three things in it:

  • Start My AI: opens your AI. Once a day it first checks whether Ollama or your model has an update or a security fix, and tells you in plain English.
  • Stop My AI: shuts it all down and gives your memory back.
  • Everything: a shortcut to your local-ai folder, with your plan, your cheat sheet, and by-hand uninstall steps in case you ever want out without a setup AI.

They never install anything by themselves, and there's no AI inside them. Just small, boring, readable scripts. Boring is good here.

  • One click on, one click off
  • Stop frees your memory
  • Checks for updates once a day
  • Only tells you, never installs
  • Works fine when you're offline
✓

Checkpoint. There's a My Local AI folder on your desktop. Open your AI with Start My AI, and hit Stop My AI when you're done so it stops eating your memory.

Later on

When it says there's an update

  • Ollama update or security fix: no setup AI needed. Use Ollama's own update button, exactly how your Start My AI message describes. Do this one quickly. Security fixes are the whole point. Ollama ships updates most weeks, so you'll see this message a lot. That's normal, not an emergency.
  • Your model's file changed online: the copy on your computer isn't affected, so you don't have to do anything. Want the new one anyway? Don't just re-download it. Paste the Update Me prompt below into your setup AI, so the new file gets the same checks as the original.
  • Your model's page was removed: that can mean someone found a problem with it. Stop using it and run the Update Me prompt.
  • Cancelled your Claude or ChatGPT subscription? No stress. Your current model keeps working fine. Ollama updates don't need a setup AI. You only need one again if you want a newer model.
09

Step nine · Optional, but sick

Build your own app

The Ollama app works. But you can have your own ChatGPT-style app that runs in your browser, lives on your computer, and never sends your chats anywhere. Your setup AI builds the whole thing. You just say yes a lot.

  • Sidebar: new chat, search, rename, delete, settings.
  • Chat and Developer modes: Developer lets the AI read folders you pick. Read only, it can't change a thing.
  • Drop in files: PDFs, pictures, text and code. Pictures need a model that can see. If yours can't, the app tells you.
  • Context meter top right, so you know when a chat is getting too long.
  • Side chats for quick side quests without messing up your main chat.
  • Voice typing that's turned into text on your computer, not in the cloud.
  • Settings: General, Skills (empty for now, more on that in a later video), Model, Generation, Developer and Usage.

Heads up: this is the biggest step. It can take an hour or more, and it eats a good chunk of your Claude or ChatGPT usage. If the AI stops halfway, type "keep going". If it says you've hit your usage limit, that's normal on the cheap plan: wait for it to reset, then type "keep going". It builds in parts, so you see each part working. It will also ask permission a lot. Keep hitting allow once. The moment you're tempted to pick "always allow" is exactly the moment the seatbelt matters.

  • Only your computer can open it
  • No web access, no tracking
  • Reads only folders you pick
  • Voice stays on your computer
  • Works offline
✓

Checkpoint. Hit Start My AI and your own app opens in your browser. Hit Stop My AI when you're done. The Skills tab is empty for now. That's coming in a later video.

Step ten · Only if you want out

The eject button

Hopefully you never need this. But knowing you can cleanly rip it all out is half of why it's safe to try. No leftover junk, no mystery background process, no 20 GB file haunting your hard drive.

The AI first makes a list of everything this project put on your computer and how much space it takes. You pick what goes. Things go to the Trash (or Recycle Bin) where possible, so you can change your mind until you empty it.

Already fired the contractor? You don't have to re-subscribe just to leave. UNINSTALL.md in your local-ai folder (written in step 8) has the do-it-by-hand version.

  • Shows you everything first
  • You pick what goes
  • Trash, not instant delete
  • Official uninstall steps
  • Proves nothing's left running
✓

Checkpoint. It's gone, clean. Empty the Trash when you're sure. And if your Claude or ChatGPT subscription is still running, cancel it in their app if you don't need it anymore.
